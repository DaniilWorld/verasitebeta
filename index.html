Why your brain resists "big changes"

Your brain evolved to protect your homeostasis. It is wired to preserve the status quo at all costs. This is why any change, even a "good" one, is automatically flagged as a threat to your internal balance.

Habits that hurt us are often the hardest to break. Because of how our neural pathways are formed, unwanted behaviors become deeply "sticky." You don’t need more discipline to change them. You need to understand the neurophysiology of how a habit is actually built and dismantled.

We plan our time, but we forget to plan our energy. Having an hour for the gym after work means nothing if your nervous system is depleted or your hormonal cycle is at a low point. You cannot build sustainable habits on a foundation of exhaustion. A stressed brain will always default to the path of least resistance.

The truth is you don’t need more discipline. You need a strategy that works even when your energy is at zero.
